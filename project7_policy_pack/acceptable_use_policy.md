# Acceptable Use Policy

Version 1.0. Date 2026-10-13. Owner: Information Security Officer. Review: every year.

This is a template written for a portfolio. It has not been reviewed by a lawyer and no organization has adopted it. Adapt it to your own business, laws and tools before using it.

## Purpose and scope

This policy tells you how to use company systems, accounts and data safely. It applies to every employee, contractor and vendor who uses them.

## You may

1. Use company systems for your work.
2. Use them for brief, reasonable personal tasks that do not break this policy, slow the network or put data at risk.

## You must

1. Use your own account and multi factor authentication. Never share a password, code or token.
2. Use the company password manager with long, unique passwords.
3. Lock your screen when you step away and install updates when asked.
4. Handle data by its level in the Data Classification Standard.
5. Report a lost device, a phishing message or a suspected incident the same day. Good faith reports are never punished.
6. Use only approved software, cloud services and AI tools for company data.

## You must not

1. Bypass or turn off security controls such as encryption, filters, antivirus or MFA.
2. Install unapproved software or connect unapproved devices to the company network.
3. Put Confidential or Restricted data into personal email, personal cloud storage or unapproved AI tools.
4. Harass anyone, or view, store or share unlawful or offensive material on company systems.
5. Probe, scan or test systems you do not own or are not authorized to test.

## Monitoring

The company may monitor the use of its systems for security and legal reasons, as the law allows. Do not expect privacy on company systems.

## Exceptions

If you cannot follow a rule for a good business reason:

1. Ask before you act. Send the security team a written request that says what, why and for how long.
2. Security reviews the risk and may require extra controls.
3. The data owner and the Information Security Officer both approve. An exception for Restricted data also needs an executive's approval.
4. Every exception has an end date of no more than 12 months, is logged in the risk register and is reviewed when it expires.

## Enforcement

Violations are reviewed by the security team and the person's manager. The outcome depends on intent, impact and history. It can range from a reminder or retraining, to loss of access, to formal discipline up to termination. Vendors face the remedies in their contracts. Unlawful acts may be reported to authorities. Retaliation against anyone who reports in good faith is not allowed.

## Acknowledgement

Everyone confirms in writing that they have read this policy when they join and again every year.
